## The Master Rule

Make something people want, then use growth, retention, revenue, and user behavior to prove it.

Everything else is subordinate:

- Fundraising is subordinate.
- Launches are subordinate.
- Hiring is subordinate.
- Press is subordinate.
- Partnerships are subordinate.
- Strategy is subordinate.
- Polish is subordinate.

## Stage 0: Pre-Idea Or Idea Search

Goal:

- Find a real problem that can become a fast-growing company.

YC/PG advice:

- Notice problems from lived experience.
- Be near technological or behavioral change.
- Prefer ideas that look non-obvious to outsiders but urgent to insiders.
- Beware ideas with no reachable early users.

Diagnostics:

- What changed recently?
- Why are you unusually close to this problem?
- Who already has this pain?
- What workaround proves urgency?
- Could this become huge if it worked?

Assignment:

- Interview 10 people with the suspected problem.
- Write down the current workaround and the cost of not solving it.

## Stage 1: Problem Validation

Goal:

- Prove that a narrow set of people urgently wants a solution.

YC/PG advice:

- Do not ask for compliments.
- Ask about past behavior.
- Look for people already spending money, time, social capital, or effort.
- A small group that loves the product beats a broad group that is mildly interested.

Diagnostics:

- Who has the most acute pain?
- What do they do now?
- What will they do if nothing changes?
- How fast can we get them to use something?

Assignment:

- Find 10-20 high-need prospects.
- Ask for concrete next steps: pilot, payment, intro, data, workflow access, or repeated use.

## Stage 2: MVP

Goal:

- Ship the smallest product that produces real behavior.

YC/PG advice:

- Launch quickly.
- Use a 90/10 solution where possible.
- Manual backends and concierge workflows are valid early.
- Perfectionism is often procrastination.

Diagnostics:

- What is the smallest useful user action?
- What can be manual?
- What must be real?
- What evidence will this MVP generate?

Assignment:

- Ship to a narrow group this week.
- Personally onboard every user.
- Record where users get confused, delighted, blocked, or indifferent.

## Stage 3: First Users

Goal:

- Recruit and learn from early users manually.

YC/PG advice:

- You cannot wait for users to arrive.
- Founders should sell and support first users themselves.
- Early users should feel unusually well cared for.
- Narrow markets can burn hotter.

Diagnostics:

- Who are the next 10 users by name?
- What manual action would make them successful?
- Are users returning or merely trying once?
- What would make the product feel made specifically for them?

Assignment:

- Recruit users one by one.
- Schedule follow-up calls with every active early user.
- Build from observed behavior, not feature requests alone.

## Stage 4: Product-Market Fit Search

Goal:

- Discover a segment where retention, pull, and willingness to pay are unmistakable.

YC/PG advice:

- Growth is the result of a product people want, not a substitute for it.
- Do not scale a product that users do not retain.
- Choose users as deliberately as users choose you.
- Fire customers who distort the product or consume learning capacity.

Diagnostics:

- Which segment loves it most?
- What cohort retains?
- Who is disappointed if this disappears?
- Which customers produce learning and revenue rather than drag?

Assignment:

- Segment users by intensity of need.
- Double down on the group with the highest love and fastest feedback.
- Stop building for low-signal users.

## Stage 5: Growth

Goal:

- Make growth a repeatable operating system.

YC/PG advice:

- Track weekly growth.
- Compound small weekly improvements.
- Growth should be tied to user value.
- Avoid vanity metrics.

Diagnostics:

- What is the one growth metric that matters this week?
- What is the growth rate?
- Which channel or loop created the growth?
- Is retention strong enough for more acquisition?

Assignment:

- Run one growth experiment per cycle.
- Write expected result before starting.
- Measure by cohort or funnel step.

## Stage 6: Revenue And Unit Economics

Goal:

- Prove that customers value the product enough to support a real business.

YC/PG advice:

- Unit economics matter.
- Growth with bad unit economics can destroy the company faster.
- Revenue is strong evidence, but not all revenue is equal.
- Early custom contracts can distract if they turn the company into consulting.

Diagnostics:

- Who pays?
- Why do they pay?
- What does it cost to acquire and serve them?
- Does each customer improve or worsen the product direction?

Assignment:

- Map pricing, conversion, gross margin, onboarding cost, and support cost.
- Identify whether revenue is repeatable or one-off.

## Stage 7: Fundraising

Goal:

- Raise quickly when the company is ready and the capital helps growth.

YC/PG advice:

- Be in fundraising mode or not.
- Investors are often ambiguous; track actions.
- Talk to investors in parallel.
- Get the first real commitment.
- Close committed money.
- Valuation is not success.

Diagnostics:

- Why raise now?
- What proof makes the company investable?
- Are you default alive or default dead?
- What happens if the round fails?

Assignment:

- Build an investor pipeline.
- Score each investor by expected value.
- Define next action for every conversation.
- Stop fundraising when the round is done and return to company building.

## Stage 8: Hiring

Goal:

- Hire only when there is a known repeatable job and founder time is the bottleneck.

YC/PG advice:

- Early overhiring can kill startups.
- Hiring does not fix lack of product pull.
- Great founders resist hiring as a vanity signal.

Diagnostics:

- What exactly would this person do every week?
- Have founders proven the job manually?
- Does hiring increase speed or complexity?
- Can the company afford the burn if growth slows?

Assignment:

- Write the repeatable task list.
- Delay any hire whose role is still exploratory founder work.

## Stage 9: Founder Team

Goal:

- Preserve trust, speed, and honesty among founders.

YC/PG advice:

- Founder conflict is a major company killer.
- Open communication matters more than founders expect.
- Mean behavior is costly.

Diagnostics:

- Are there unresolved disagreements?
- Are responsibilities clear?
- Is one founder avoiding the hardest work?
- Can founders tell each other bad news quickly?

Assignment:

- Hold a founder-only operating review.
- Name the hardest unspoken concern.
- Clarify who owns each critical problem this cycle.

## Cross-Cutting Failure Modes

Watch for:

- Building without users.
- Talking to users but not learning.
- Compliments mistaken for demand.
- Launch as theater.
- Partnerships as procrastination.
- Fundraising as avoidance.
- Hiring before pull.
- Too many priorities.
- User segment too broad.
- Weak retention hidden by acquisition.
- Burn increasing faster than learning.
- Investor interest mistaken for commitment.
- Founder disagreement left unnamed.
