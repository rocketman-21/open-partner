# Recreating YC-Style Office Hours

Last updated: 2026-05-12

This is not YC. It is a YC-inspired operating model for founder + Codex sessions every few days.

Primary sources:

- PG, What I've Learned from Users: https://www.paulgraham.com/users.html
- PG, Do Things that Don't Scale: https://www.paulgraham.com/ds.html
- PG, Startup = Growth: https://www.paulgraham.com/growth.html
- YC, What Happens at YC: https://www.ycombinator.com/about/
- YC, Essential Startup Advice: https://www.ycombinator.com/blog/ycs-essential-startup-advice/

## The Point Of Office Hours

The point is not to produce advice. The point is to increase founder focus so the company moves faster before the next session.

The session succeeds if we leave with:

- A clearer diagnosis.
- One priority.
- One or two concrete actions.
- A measurable expected result.
- A date for review.

## The Default Session Shape

Length: 45 minutes.

1. Facts since last session - 5 minutes.
2. User learning - 10 minutes.
3. Metrics and runway - 5 minutes.
4. Problem diagnosis - 10 minutes.
5. Priority selection - 5 minutes.
6. Experiment design - 7 minutes.
7. Commitments and red flags - 3 minutes.

## Input Required Before Each Session

Use `templates/founder-update.md`.

Minimum facts:

- What shipped.
- Who used it.
- What users did.
- What users said.
- Revenue or sales progress.
- Growth/retention metrics.
- Burn/runway if relevant.
- Biggest worry.
- Proposed priority.

If the input is missing, office hours starts by reconstructing facts, not by discussing strategy.

## The YC-Style Diagnostic Pattern

When a founder brings a problem, ask:

- Is this the real problem or a symptom?
- Is this problem existential, important, or merely annoying?
- Is the founder avoiding a harder problem?
- Can we solve or test it within a week?
- What evidence would change our mind?

Examples:

- "Fundraising is hard" may really mean growth is weak.
- "User acquisition is hard" may really mean the product is not compelling.
- "We need to hire" may really mean founders are avoiding sales or support.
- "We need a big launch" may really mean we do not know how to recruit users manually.
- "We need more features" may really mean we have not found the acute user segment.

## Priority Rules

Only one primary priority per cycle.

Rank by:

1. Company-killing risk.
2. User demand proof.
3. Growth or revenue leverage.
4. Speed of learning.
5. Reversibility.

Default priorities by stage:

- No product: user interviews and problem selection.
- MVP exists: launch to narrow users and manually onboard.
- Users exist but weak retention: find why people stop.
- Retention exists but weak growth: find repeatable acquisition.
- Revenue exists but sales slow: founder-led sales process and pipeline.
- Fundraising mode: investor pipeline, proof, and explicit commitments.
- Post-raise: avoid overhiring and preserve speed.

## The One-Week Experiment Standard

Every action should be small enough to review soon.

Experiment must include:

- Hypothesis.
- Target users.
- Action.
- Metric.
- Expected outcome.
- Failure signal.
- Owner.
- Deadline.

Good:

- "Personally onboard 10 design partners from the security team lead list and measure how many import data within 24 hours."

Bad:

- "Work on growth."

## User Learning Standard

Strong user learning:

- Names the user segment.
- Describes past behavior.
- Captures the user's actual workflow.
- Identifies urgency or indifference.
- Includes a surprise.
- Changes the product or go-to-market plan.

Weak user learning:

- "People liked it."
- "They said they would pay."
- "They asked for more features."
- "We got good feedback."

## Metrics Standard

Pick the fewest metrics that describe truth:

- Activation: did a user reach the first value moment?
- Retention: did they come back?
- Revenue: did they pay?
- Growth: are more people doing the thing each week?
- Sales: are prospects moving to the next concrete step?
- Runway: can the company survive long enough to learn?

If the company is pre-product, the metric may be interviews, pilots, or active users. The key is that it must produce evidence, not merely activity.

## Codex's Role In These Sessions

Codex should behave like:

- A pattern-recognition partner.
- A focus enforcer.
- A user-learning interrogator.
- A one-week experiment designer.
- A memory keeper for commitments and prior hypotheses.

Codex should not behave like:

- A motivational poster.
- A generic consultant.
- A yes-person.
- A long-form strategy memo machine when a founder needs action.

## Session Output Format

At the end of each session, produce:

```md
# Office Hours Summary - YYYY-MM-DD

## Diagnosis

## One Priority

## Actions Before Next Session

## Metrics To Report Back

## Risks / Watchouts

## Next Session Question
```

## Cadence

For "every few days," use:

- Short office hours every 2-3 days.
- One deeper weekly review.
- One monthly reset on strategy, runway, and growth model.

Short sessions should not become therapy or status theater. They should decide the next hard action.
