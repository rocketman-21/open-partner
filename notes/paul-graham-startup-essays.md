# Paul Graham Startup Essays - Applied Notes

Last updated: 2026-05-12

This file is a synthesis for practical use. It links to source essays rather than storing full text.

## Core Startup Definition

Source: https://www.paulgraham.com/growth.html

PG's startup definition centers on rapid growth. A startup is not merely a young company, a tech company, or a venture-backed company. It is a company designed to grow fast.

Office-hours implication:

- Ask what the current growth engine is.
- Ask what weekly growth rate would count as real progress.
- Separate "interesting project" from "company designed for fast growth."
- Use growth as a decision compass, but do not force growth before people actually want the product.

## Do Things That Do Not Scale

Source: https://www.paulgraham.com/ds.html

The early company should not wait for scalable channels. Founders often need to recruit users manually, over-serve them, build concierge workflows, narrow the market, and learn through direct contact.

Office-hours implication:

- The founder should name the next 10 users or customers, not only the channel.
- The founder should be willing to sell, onboard, support, and observe users manually.
- A launch is not a substitute for recruiting users.
- Manual service is not a failure if it teaches the product what automation should become.

## Learn From Users

Source: https://www.paulgraham.com/users.html

The best short advice for YC applicants is to explain what they learned from users. That single question tests attention, need, insight, and founder quality.

Office-hours implication:

- Every session starts with user learning.
- "We shipped X" matters less than "users did Y after we shipped X."
- Strong user learning includes surprise, specificity, and behavior.
- Weak user learning is mostly compliments, hypotheticals, or generic market opinion.

## Startup Ideas

Source: https://www.paulgraham.com/startupideas.html

Strong startup ideas often begin as organic observations from people living near the future. The best ideas can look small, strange, or like chores from the outside because only some people can see the need clearly yet.

Office-hours implication:

- Ask whether the founders personally understand the problem.
- Ask what changed recently to make the idea possible or urgent.
- Ask why other people have missed or avoided it.
- Prefer lived pain and specific user pull over abstract market maps.

## Survival And Runway

Sources:

- https://www.paulgraham.com/aord.html
- https://www.paulgraham.com/pinch.html
- https://www.paulgraham.com/ramenprofitable.html

Founders should know whether the company is default alive or default dead. The fatal danger is slow growth plus insufficient runway plus the assumption that investors will save the company.

Office-hours implication:

- Ask for cash, burn, runway, revenue, and growth.
- Separate plan A from plan B.
- Do not let hiring or spend become a substitute for product appeal.
- Survival creates optionality; low burn can be a strategic advantage.

## Fundraising

Sources:

- https://www.paulgraham.com/fr.html
- https://www.paulgraham.com/convince.html
- https://www.paulgraham.com/fundraising.html
- https://www.paulgraham.com/hiresfund.html

Fundraising is distracting and should be treated as a mode. Investors respond to growth, evidence, founder quality, and other investor interest. Ambiguous investor language should be treated cautiously until commitments are explicit.

Office-hours implication:

- Do not let fundraising flatten the growth curve for too long.
- Decide explicitly whether the company is in fundraising mode.
- Track investor process by actions, not positive words.
- Close committed money quickly.
- Keep the story simple: what changed, why now, why this team, why users need it, why it can become huge.

## Failure Modes

Sources:

- https://www.paulgraham.com/startupmistakes.html
- https://www.paulgraham.com/die.html
- https://www.paulgraham.com/startuplessons.html
- https://www.paulgraham.com/13sentences.html

Repeated startup failure patterns include building something people do not want, weak founders/founder conflict, choosing a too-small or bad market, slow launch, poor user contact, bad hiring timing, and distraction.

Office-hours implication:

- Look for the failure mode hidden underneath the founder's stated concern.
- Ask whether the current work proves demand or merely improves polish.
- Watch for founder conflict early.
- Penalize vague progress.

## YC, Peers, And Founder Clusters

Sources:

- https://www.paulgraham.com/whyyc.html
- https://www.paulgraham.com/ycstart.html
- https://www.paulgraham.com/ycombinator.html
- https://www.paulgraham.com/hubs.html

YC's power is not just advice. It is a dense cluster of ambitious peers, repeated pattern recognition, investor attention, and a culture where founders help each other.

Office-hours implication:

- Recreate peer pressure with visible commitments and short cadence.
- Recreate pattern recognition by keeping a taxonomy of recurring problems.
- Recreate community by asking for intros, customer routes, and feedback loops.
- Recreate ambition calibration by asking how big this could get if the founders did the right things.
