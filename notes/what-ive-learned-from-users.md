# What I've Learned from Users - Office-Hours Distillation

Source: https://www.paulgraham.com/users.html

This note distills the essay and the excerpt provided in chat. It does not reproduce the full essay.

## Core Thesis

YC works partly because early startups have many recurring problems, but individual startups still need individualized advice. The repeat pattern gives partners a diagnostic advantage; the company-specific context makes office hours necessary.

## What YC Learns By Seeing Many Startups

- Startups often have the same underlying problems in different disguises.
- Early-stage investing/advising creates more data because more things go wrong.
- The advice cannot be reduced to a formula because the problem ranking differs by company.
- Founders often bring in the symptom, not the root problem.
- Founders are often wrong about which problem is most important.

## Office Hours As A Diagnostic Loop

The practical loop:

1. Find the real problem.
2. Rank the problems by existential importance.
3. Choose the highest-leverage action.
4. Make the action small enough to test in days or a week.
5. Return with measured results.
6. Correct course quickly.

The interesting part is not a partner handing out generic wisdom. It is the partner noticing that the founder is worried about the wrong thing.

## Why Founders Often Do Not Listen

- Startup advice often sounds wrong because startups are counterintuitive.
- Founders have been trained to win in school-like environments where optimizing the test can work.
- Startups punish fake progress and reward direct engagement with reality.
- Founders need repeated contact with consequences before some advice becomes believable.

## The Focus Equation

The essay's operating logic:

- Speed defines startups.
- Focus enables speed.
- YC improves focus.

That should become the spine of our mock office hours. The session is successful if the founder leaves more focused and can move faster before the next session.

## Recreating The YC Effect

We cannot recreate YC's full network, investor brand, or batch density. We can recreate parts of the advising loop:

- Ask for concrete facts rather than narratives.
- Look for symptoms that point to deeper causes.
- Choose one main problem per cycle.
- Demand user evidence.
- Set near-term experiments.
- Review results quickly.
- Keep the founder from hiding in fake work.

## Questions To Use In Mock Office Hours

- What did you learn from users since the last session?
- Which users did something, not merely say something?
- What problem are you bringing in, and what might be the deeper problem underneath it?
- If we could fix only one thing before the next session, what would most change the company's trajectory?
- Are you trying to solve a user problem, an investor problem, a founder-confidence problem, or an optics problem?
- What is the smallest action that would produce new evidence in less than a week?
- What result would make us change direction?
