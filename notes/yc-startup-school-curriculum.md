# YC and Startup School Curriculum Themes

Last updated: 2026-05-12

Primary sources:

- Startup School: https://www.startupschool.org

## Recurring Curriculum Loop

YC's public curriculum repeatedly returns to a simple loop:

1. Pick a real problem.
2. Build the smallest useful thing.
3. Launch quickly.
4. Talk to users.
5. Measure behavior.
6. Iterate.
7. Repeat until growth and retention show real pull.

## Theme: Idea Quality

Common questions:

- Who has the problem?
- How often do they have it?
- How painful is it?
- What are they doing today instead?
- Why will this become more urgent over time?
- Why is this team unusually likely to notice or solve it?

Office-hours assignment:

- Write a one-sentence customer/problem statement.
- List 10 specific people or companies with the problem.
- Interview 5 of them before the next session.

## Theme: MVP

YC's MVP advice is usually not "build a bad product." It is closer to: build the smallest thing that creates real utility and lets users behave.

Office-hours assignment:

- Identify the smallest useful action a user can complete.
- Remove every feature that does not help that action.
- Launch to a narrow group.

## Theme: Talking To Users

Good user conversations are not pitch sessions. They are evidence gathering.

Look for:

- Past behavior.
- Current workaround.
- Cost of the problem.
- Urgency.
- Buying process.
- Emotional language.
- Actual use after launch.

Avoid:

- Asking whether they "would use" a hypothetical product.
- Over-weighting compliments.
- Asking users to design the product for you.
- Talking only to friends who want to be supportive.

## Theme: Growth

Growth should be the result of making something people want, not a cosmetic metric. Pre-product-market-fit growth can amplify churn and cash burn.

Good early growth questions:

- Are users returning?
- Are users bringing other users?
- Are users paying or showing equivalent commitment?
- Are we growing because a channel is working or because the product is loved?
- Which small group loves it most?

## Theme: Metrics

Founders should choose metrics that reflect real user value. Vanity metrics are dangerous because they let the company feel active while avoiding the core problem.

Core startup metrics:

- Active users or active accounts.
- Retention.
- Revenue.
- Growth rate by week.
- Conversion by funnel step.
- Sales pipeline, if B2B.
- Burn, runway, and default alive/dead status.

## Theme: Sales

YC advice for early B2B companies often collapses into founder-led sales:

- The founders sell first.
- Sales conversations are product discovery.
- A design partner should expose urgency and workflow, not become a custom consulting trap.
- A few high-need customers beat many low-need leads.

## Theme: Launch

Launches are useful for finding initial users and learning, not because a launch itself creates a company.

Office-hours assignment:

- Launch earlier and narrower than feels comfortable.
- Define the user action that counts.
- Follow up manually with every serious user.

## Theme: Fundraising

The public YC fundraising curriculum emphasizes speed, clarity, and avoiding ambiguous investor process.

Questions:

- Are we ready to raise, or would raising now waste time?
- Are we in fundraising mode or not?
- What proof makes this round attractive?
- Which investors are highest expected value?
- What is the next concrete investor action?

## Theme: Founder Psychology

YC often warns founders about fake work:

- Conferences.
- Partnerships that do not produce users.
- Press before product pull.
- Hiring as a way to avoid product work.
- Fundraising when the company is not ready.
- Excessive polish before launch.

The office-hours job is to make the real work unavoidable.
