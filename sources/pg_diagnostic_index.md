# PG Diagnostic Index

**Rule zero — incompressibility.** Paul Graham's essays are incompressible:
there is load-bearing information in nearly every sentence, and every summary
loses some of it. This index exists ONLY for routing — find the test that fits
the founder's situation, then **read the entire essay file** in
`sources/paul_graham_essays/markdown/` before applying it. Quoting the essay
beats paraphrasing it; paraphrasing it beats this index; this index alone is
malpractice.

Entries are grouped by the workflow's bottleneck gates
(`playbooks/pg_yc_office_hours_workflow.md`). Format:
**test name** (`essay file`) — one-line pointer.

## Gate 1 — Survival

- **Default alive** (`063-aord.md`) — at constant expenses and current revenue
  growth, do you reach profitability on the money you have? The rest of the
  session depends on this answer; half of founders past month 8 can't give it.
- **Fatal pinch** (`073-pinch.md`) — default dead + slow growth + <6 months
  runway + plan is "raise more." Say the clairvoyance line: the probability of
  that raise is zero. Then: shrink if overhired; if small, everyone sells.
- **Ramen profitability** (`116-ramenprofitable.md`, `037-airbnbs.md`) — revenue
  covering founders' living expenses flips the future's default state from
  dying to surviving; the Airbnbs taped $4,000/mo to the bathroom mirror.
- **Infinite runway** (`130-divergence.md`) — crossing profitability at any
  level, even $3k/mo, is a qualitative change in your negotiating position.
- **Cockroach test** (`133-badeconomy.md`, `156-guidetoinvestors.md`) — the
  cheaper you are to run, the harder you are to kill; threats live in the
  mirror, not the news.

## Gate 2 — Real user demand

- **The single best question** (`036-ace.md`) — "How do you know people want
  this?" If PG had to fund on one question, it would be this one.
- **Larval market** (`036-ace.md`) — some people must want it so urgently
  RIGHT NOW that they'll use a buggy v1 from two unknowns. Its absence is the
  biggest flaw in most startup ideas.
- **Sitcom-idea test** (`081-startupideas.md`) — "yeah, maybe I could see using
  that" summed across the whole population = zero users.
- **Well, not crater** (`081-startupideas.md`) — a small number of people who
  want it a large amount beats a large number who want it a little; then
  demand a fast path out of the small market.
- **Would you use it yourself?** (`081-startupideas.md`, `107-organic.md`,
  `019-users.md`) — asked constantly at YC; "you'd be surprised how often the
  answer is no."
- **Toy test** (`081-startupideas.md`, `107-organic.md`) — dismissed as a toy =
  has everything an idea needs except being important, which follows.
- **Sell before you build** (`081-startupideas.md`, Buchheit) — founders with
  bad ideas should go try to sell the product first; they return with a real
  idea found while failing to sell the fake one.
- **Schlep blindness** (`091-schlep.md`) — ask what problem you wish someone
  else would solve for you; your unconscious hid the painful (valuable) ideas.
- **Proxy for demand** (`165-investors.md`, Sequoia) — what are people doing
  NOW with inadequate tools that proves the need?

## Gate 3 — Product value / activation

- **Launching teaches you what you should have been building**
  (`127-13sentences.md`, `109-really.md`) — the initial version is a trick for
  getting users to start talking to you; the conversation doesn't start until
  you launch.
- **Troll-launch threshold** (`109-really.md`) — if v1 was so polished that
  trolls didn't mock it, you waited too long.
- **Five-minute test drive** (`221-road.md`) — can a user reach a real working
  result in five minutes? One message at the funnel's confusion point took
  Viaweb's completion from 60% to 90%.
- **Doctor's rule** (`213-desres.md`) — users report symptoms; you diagnose.
  Build what the user needs, not what he asks for.
- **Support-as-QA** (`221-road.md`) — support thirty feet from the programmers;
  a bug report is someone bringing important news.
- **Finished-product test** (`171-startuplessons.md`) — "it seems finished"
  means either it is, or you lack imagination — and it's the second, a
  thousand to one.

## Gate 4 — Retention

- **Ten users who love you** (`127-13sentences.md`, `121-5founders.md`) —
  better to make a few users love you than many ambivalent; if everything
  collapsed tomorrow, who would be genuinely upset?
- **What do they love?** (`151-die.md`) — find the small group that loves you,
  name precisely what they love, do more of exactly that.
- **Tamagotchi effect** (`139-good.md`) — having users to take care of forces
  you to learn what makes them happy and keeps you working when you feel
  doomed.

## Gate 5 — Growth / distribution

- **Weekly growth rate, the only number** (`083-growth.md`) — "a hundred new
  customers a month" is not a rate. 5–7%/wk is good, 10% exceptional, 1% means
  you haven't figured out what you're doing yet. Hit it this week or you
  failed at the only thing that mattered this week.
- **Growth as compass** (`083-growth.md`) — whatever hits the target rate is
  ipso facto right; let the rate make decisions and the company evolves into
  a better one than you planned.
- **10%-a-week arithmetic** (`079-ds.md`) — 100 users growing 10%/wk is 2
  million in 2 years; judge progress by rate, never by absolute count.
- **Do things that don't scale** (`079-ds.md`) — recruit users manually
  (nearly universal), Collison installation ("give me your laptop"), consult
  for one user, be your own factory.
- **Contained fire** (`079-ds.md`) — find the subset where you can reach
  critical mass fast (Facebook: one campus); ask where demand is hottest and
  pour effort there (`037-airbnbs.md`: NYC).
- **You make what you measure** (`127-13sentences.md`, Kraus) — plot users on
  the wall daily; merely measuring a number improves it, so choose carefully.
- **Extraordinary-effort test** (`079-ds.md`) — any growth strategy that omits
  founder sweat (the big launch, the partnership) is ipso facto suspect.

## Gate 6 — Focus / founder behavior

- **Shower test** (`105-top.md`) — what do your thoughts drift to? You can only
  do a great job on the top idea in your mind, and money/disputes are
  velcro-shaped thoughts that displace it.
- **Type-B procrastination** (`177-procrastination.md`) — the most dangerous
  kind doesn't feel like procrastination: you're getting things done — the
  wrong things. The to-do list can itself be the avoidance.
- **Fake-work alarm** (`106-selfindulgence.md`) — the worst time sinks mimic
  virtuous work and aren't even fun.
- **Maker's schedule** (`117-makersschedule.md`) — one meeting blows an
  afternoon; count your unbroken half-days.
- **Persistent vs obstinate** (`009-persistence.md`) — attached to the goal vs
  attached to your ideas about reaching it. Watch for predatory listening:
  does criticism make them lean in, or do their eyes glaze?
- **Running back** (`098-founders.md`, `171-startuplessons.md`) — determined to
  get downfield, willing to go sideways or backwards; the one thing a running
  back never does is stand still.
- **Word to the resourceful** (`090-word.md`) — give a founder a one-line lead;
  the good ones chase down every implication unprompted.
- **Playing house** (`075-before.md`) — every outward form of a startup except
  making something people want. There is no boss to trick, only users.
- **The "but" test** (`151-die.md`) — any sentence ending "...but we're going
  to keep working on the startup" means they've already given up.
- **Going dark** (`151-die.md`) — two months of silence is the #1 death
  predictor; not answering email has been 100% accurate so far.
- **Mean makes stupid** (`074-mean.md`) — startups don't win by attacking;
  they win by transcending.
- **Two opposing ideas** (`224-popular.md`) — "how hard can it be?" about the
  problem and "this will never work" about your current solution,
  simultaneously.

## Fundraising (route here ONLY when it is truly the constraint)

- **Type A vs Type B** (`076-fr.md`) — raising when you don't need it vs when
  you do; the best fundraising move is reaching the point of not needing it.
- **Hear no till yes** (`076-fr.md`, `156-guidetoinvestors.md`) — every
  investor is a no until money wires or a no-contingency offer is in writing.
  "We're really excited, keep in touch" = no. No termsheet in hand = no.
- **Deals fall through** (`134-fundraising.md`, `179-startupfunding.md`) —
  "Birds fly; fish swim; deals fall through." Run the company as if every
  deal didn't exist; nothing makes a deal close like not needing it.
- **Won't-lead translation** (`076-fr.md`, `123-angelinvesting.md`) — "I want
  to invest but don't lead" = "no, except yes if you turn out to be hot."
  Expected value: zero.
- **Breadth-first by expected value** (`076-fr.md`) — all investors in
  parallel; flaky ones deprioritize themselves like failing processors.
- **Equity equation** (`154-equity.md`) — give up n% only if it improves your
  average outcome by more than 1/(1-n). You should feel richer after every
  equity trade.
- **Corp-dev barbell** (`069-corpdev.md`) — talk to acquirers only when doing
  really well or really badly. "Do you want to sell the company right now?"
  No? Then don't take the meeting.
- **Fundraising is not the test** (`050-lesson.md`, `076-fr.md`,
  `084-swan.md`) — pride in a raise is pride in your college grades; the
  market is the unhackable test, and "if it's not a good investment, why are
  you even doing it?"
- **Top-idea cost** (`105-top.md`) — the real price of fundraising is that it
  becomes the top idea in your mind and the company grinds to a halt. One
  founder raises; the others build.
- **Rejection is a question** (`156-guidetoinvestors.md`) — not an answer.
  "Do we suck?" is yours to investigate, not theirs to decree.

## Idea stage (pre-startup sessions)

- **Organic vs made-up** (`107-organic.md`, `081-startupideas.md`) — the verb
  is *notice*, not *think up*. Live in the future, build what's missing. The
  worst YC ideas are young founders building for users unlike themselves.
- **Why now?** (`078-convince.md`) — if it's a great idea, why hasn't someone
  done it? Ideal answer: something just changed, and nobody else noticed yet.
- **Bad idea that isn't** (`038-think.md`, `084-swan.md`) — good startup ideas
  sound like bad ideas to most people; if everyone agrees it's good, others
  are already doing it.
- **What Microsoft is this the Altair Basic of?** (`066-altair.md`) — turn a
  lame-sounding idea into a puzzle before dismissing it.
- **Deceptively small bridgehead** (`089-ambitious.md`) — never announce the
  giant ambition; ship the Harvard-undergrads version and expand westward.
- **Crowded-market thesis** (`081-startupideas.md`) — a crowded market is fine
  with a precise thesis about what incumbents overlook; "worried we're too
  late" is itself a sign of a good idea.
- **Something odd** (`226-avg.md`) — average practice means average
  performance, and average startup performance is death. What are you doing
  that's odd, and what does it let you do that competitors literally can't?

## Cofounders & people

- **Dog and sock** (`098-founders.md`, `109-really.md`) — startups pull
  founder relationships apart if they can be pulled apart; how long have you
  known each other and what have you survived together?
- **Relentlessly resourceful** (`122-relres.md`) — the defining founder
  quality; the opposite of hapless. Apply it to yourself and anyone you'd add.
- **Animal test** (`193-start.md`) — "so-and-so is an animal": if you laugh,
  don't hire them. Only people who write code or get users
  (`163-startupmistakes.md`).
- **Trust your instincts about people** (`075-before.md`) — you can't trust
  instincts about startup mechanics, but if someone seems slippery or a jerk,
  do not ignore it.
- **Hiring is the worst thing** (`193-start.md`, `199-bubble.md`) — the
  question isn't whether you can afford the salaries; it's whether you can
  afford the productivity loss of being bigger.

## Dying / demoralized

- **How not to die** (`151-die.md`) — startups rarely die in mid keystroke, so
  keep typing; if you can just avoid dying, you get rich. Make quitting
  publicly humiliating.
- **Why haven't you given up?** (`037-airbnbs.md`) — the answer reveals
  motives; "we are not going to slow down" is seven words of signal.
- **Doom is self-fulfilling** (`139-good.md`) — feeling doomed stops the work,
  which causes the doom. Really committed + cheap to run = very hard to kill.
- **Skill × determination × luck** (`109-really.md`) — a zero luck roll zeros
  the product; failure isn't always a verdict on you.
- **Pivots should converge** (`163-startupmistakes.md`) — successive ideas
  should reuse most of what you built; restarting from scratch each time is
  the bad sign.

## Partner voice (how PG delivers it)

Read `019-users.md`, `073-pinch.md`, and `036-ace.md` for tone calibration.
The signature moves:

1. **Concrete over abstract, always.** Not "your burn is concerning" but the
   default-alive arithmetic, out loud, with their numbers.
2. **The deflating "just."** How do we convince investors? "Just grow fast."
   How do we grow fast? "Just make something people want." Looking for the
   trick IS the tell.
3. **Clairvoyance bluntness** (`073-pinch.md`): "I will now, by an amazing
   feat of clairvoyance, do this for you: the probability is zero."
4. **Relative importance surgery** (`019-users.md`): founders bring three
   problems weighted equally — one is moderate, one doesn't matter, one will
   kill them. Say which is which: "never mind about your boyfriend, think
   about that door!"
5. **Questions as scalpels.** The hardest cuts arrive as questions: "If it's
   not a good investment, why are you even doing it?"
6. **Numbers as verdicts.** 5%/wk vs 1%/wk; 1/(1-n); $4,000/mo on the mirror.
7. **Permission to be wrong about the future, never about facts.** A blurry
   vision is fine; a fuzzy growth rate is not.
