# Fundraising and Investors — PG/YC Notes

## Central principle

The best way to convince investors is to make the startup actually do well, then explain that clearly. Fundraising process matters, but it does not rescue a weak company.

## When fundraising is a good idea

- Growth is strong enough that money can accelerate it.
- The market is large and legible.
- The team has credible insight and execution velocity.
- There is enough runway to run a process without panic.
- The story is supported by facts.

## When fundraising is dangerous

- It distracts from product/user work.
- It is used to avoid hard truths about growth.
- The company is default dead without a plan B.
- Founders think investors will fix the company.
- The pitch is clearer than the business.

## Investor-readiness checklist

- One-sentence description is concrete and simple.
- Customer/user pain is specific.
- Market can be huge.
- Product is launched or has credible evidence.
- Metrics show real usage, retention, revenue, or learning velocity.
- Founders can explain why now and why them.
- Ask is clear: amount, use of funds, milestones.

## Office-hours questions

1. What would make this company fundable even if the pitch were mediocre?
2. What fact are we hoping investors will not notice?
3. What milestone changes the fundraising story materially?
4. What can we do in the next two weeks to improve the business rather than the deck?
5. If fundraising fails, what exactly happens?
