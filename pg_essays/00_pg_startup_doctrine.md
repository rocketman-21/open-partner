# Paul Graham Startup Doctrine

## The compressed version

1. A startup is a company designed to grow fast.
2. Growth comes from making something people want, not from startup theater.
3. The earliest version should get into users' hands quickly.
4. The first users should be recruited and served manually if necessary.
5. It is better to make a few users love you than many users feel indifferent.
6. Founders should measure the one or two numbers that reveal whether the company is working.
7. Spend little; survive long enough to learn.
8. Fundraising is useful when it accelerates a company already showing evidence, but it is a dangerous distraction when used as a substitute for progress.
9. Startups are counterintuitive; advice often sounds wrong until experience proves it right.
10. The founder's job is to stay honest, focused, fast, and close to users.

## The recurring anti-patterns

- Waiting for users to arrive after launch.
- Hiring before proving the product or sales motion.
- Confusing fundraising with success.
- Confusing press with traction.
- Confusing partnerships with distribution.
- Building a broad product for average users instead of a narrow product for intense users.
- Measuring vanity metrics.
- Over-polishing before launch.
- Playing house: acting like a startup instead of making something people want.

## The recurring remedies

- Talk to users.
- Launch a small useful thing.
- Recruit users manually.
- Delight early users beyond normal expectations.
- Pick a narrow early market.
- Use manual/concierge work to discover what software should automate.
- Track weekly growth and retention.
- Cut burn if default dead.
- Do one hard, concrete thing this week.
