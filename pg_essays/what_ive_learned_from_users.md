# What I've Learned from Users — YC Office-Hours Design Notes

Source: uploaded pasted text in this conversation.

## Key claims for our purposes

### 1. Startup problems recur

Most startups have different surface details but surprisingly similar problem patterns. A partner who has advised many startups develops a map of common failure modes.

Mock implication: build a reusable diagnostic map, but never let it replace company-specific context.

### 2. Advice cannot be fully automated

The same category of problem manifests differently in each company. YC learned that founders need partners who know them well.

Mock implication: maintain a persistent company profile and decision log so advice compounds over time.

### 3. Founders often misdiagnose the problem

A founder may think the issue is fundraising when the real issue is weak company performance. They may think the issue is user acquisition when the product is not good enough.

Mock implication: office hours should always ask, "Is this the real problem or a symptom?"

### 4. Relative importance matters

Founders often know some problems but mis-rank them. The partner's job is to point at the thing that will kill the company if ignored.

Mock implication: use the problem priority matrix every session.

### 5. Startup advice is counterintuitive

Founders ignore advice partly because it contradicts instincts trained by school, jobs, or social convention.

Mock implication: when advice sounds wrong, write down the experiment that could prove it either way.

### 6. Focus enables speed

The essence of YC advice: figure out which problems matter most, invent ideas to solve them, keep the resolution to a week or less where possible, try them, and measure results.

Mock implication: every session ends with an experiment, owner, metric, and check-in date.

### 7. Colleagues may be as valuable as advice

YC deliberately creates a cluster of ambitious founders who help each other and raise each other's standards.

Mock implication: use written updates and, if possible, add a small peer/advisor group to approximate the colleague effect.

## Office-hours mantra from this essay

Speed defines startups. Focus enables speed. Office hours should improve focus.
