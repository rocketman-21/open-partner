# Do Things That Don't Scale — Playbook

## Core idea

Startups usually do not take off because founders launch a product and wait. They take off because founders push them into motion. Early work often looks manual, inefficient, and embarrassingly small. That is usually the point.

## The unscalable tactics

### 1. Manually recruit users

Do not wait for users. Identify individual people or companies with the problem and ask them directly.

Office-hours check:

- Name the exact 10 users you will contact.
- Explain why they are likely early adopters.
- Define the ask: demo, install, purchase, trial, interview, referral, or setup.

### 2. Install/onboard users yourself

If a user agrees to try the product, do not merely send a link. Help them get to value immediately.

Office-hours check:

- What is the fastest path from interest to first value?
- Can the founder personally set it up?
- What part of onboarding can be concierge for now?

### 3. Delight early users

The first users should feel unusually cared for. This is not just customer service; it is a feedback engine and culture-setting mechanism.

Office-hours check:

- What would make early users tell others unprompted?
- What support would feel impossible from a big company?
- What did support conversations reveal about the product?

### 4. Start with a narrow market

A narrow market can burn hot. The goal is not smallness; it is intensity and density.

Office-hours check:

- Which subset has the strongest pain and fastest adoption cycle?
- Can they influence each other?
- Can we reach them this week?

### 5. Act like a consultant, without becoming a consultancy

For B2B, pick one user/customer and solve their problem intensely. Use the manual work to discover the generalizable product.

Office-hours check:

- What is the single user's exact workflow?
- Which parts are specific to them vs common across similar users?
- What can be repeated for the next 5 users?

### 6. Be the software manually

Do the operation by hand before automating. This lets you launch faster and learn what to automate.

Office-hours check:

- What could we fake manually behind the scenes?
- What is the minimum promise we can fulfill reliably?
- What manual bottleneck must be automated next?

## What usually does not work

- Big launches as the primary growth plan.
- Partnerships as the first distribution engine.
- Hiring sales before founders understand sales.
- Waiting until the product is polished enough to avoid embarrassment.

## Weekly sprint format

1. Pick one narrow user segment.
2. Pick 10-50 specific targets.
3. Write the outreach message.
4. Founder sends it personally.
5. Founder handles demo/onboarding personally.
6. Log every objection and moment of confusion.
7. Change product or positioning within 48 hours.
8. Report back: contacted, replied, activated, retained, paid, learned.
