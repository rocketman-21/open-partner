# Before the Startup — Counterintuitive Lessons

## Core idea

Startups are counterintuitive. Founders often ignore good advice because it sounds wrong relative to school, big-company work, or social instincts.

## Major traps

### 1. Playing house

Founders imitate startup externals: pitch decks, fundraising, office, hiring, titles, conferences. The real work is making something users want.

Diagnostic:

- What activity would still matter if nobody saw it except users?

### 2. Looking for the trick

Founders ask for tricks to raise, grow, pitch, or hire. Usually the real answer is simpler and harder: make the company actually work.

Diagnostic:

- Are we trying to optimize the test or improve the underlying reality?

### 3. Trusting instincts in a counterintuitive domain

A founder's instincts may say "wait until polished," "look bigger," "hire a salesperson," or "avoid rejection." Startup reality often says the opposite.

Diagnostic:

- Which uncomfortable action are we rationalizing away?

### 4. Over-learning startup mechanics

Legal/fundraising knowledge is useful when needed, but dangerous if it displaces user/product work.

Diagnostic:

- Are we learning mechanics because it helps this week, or because it feels safer than talking to users?

## Preparation principle

The best preparation is not entrepreneurship cosplay. It is becoming the kind of person who notices real problems, builds things, and works well with people they respect.
