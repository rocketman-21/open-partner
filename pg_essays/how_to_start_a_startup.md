# How to Start a Startup — Operating Notes

## Core triad

PG's early formulation: successful startups need good people, something customers actually want, and low spending.

## 1. Good people

Founders matter more than initial ideas because ideas change. Good founders can adapt; bad founders can waste a promising idea.

Office-hours checks:

- Can the founders build and sell the product themselves initially?
- Do they have unusual insight into the problem?
- Are they honest about what they do not know?
- Are they high-agency or waiting for permission?
- Is there cofounder trust and role clarity?

## 2. Make something customers want

Startup ideas are starting points, not fixed blueprints. The product becomes right through contact with users.

Office-hours checks:

- Who is the customer?
- What do they currently use?
- What is painful about the current solution?
- Why now?
- Why these founders?

## 3. Spend little

Low burn keeps the company alive long enough to iterate. Frugality is not a virtue-signaling austerity ritual; it is speed insurance.

Office-hours checks:

- How many months of runway?
- What is the next expensive commitment?
- What can the founders do manually before hiring?
- What spend directly produces learning or growth?

## The most important practical habit

Do not overvalue the initial idea. Use it as a hypothesis. The company is the evolving answer to user reality.
