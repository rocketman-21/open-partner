# Startup = Growth

## Core idea

A startup is not simply a new company. It is a company designed to grow fast. Growth is the defining property and the compass for decisions.

## What this implies

- A startup needs a big or rapidly expanding market.
- It needs a product that many people want, or a path from a narrow intense market to a much bigger one.
- It needs a way to reach and serve that market at scale.
- It usually faces fierce competition because large opportunities are valuable.
- It often starts with an overlooked insight: founders see a problem others do not because they live near the edge of change.

## Office-hours growth questions

1. What is the key weekly growth metric?
2. What is the current weekly growth rate?
3. What growth rate would make this undeniably exciting?
4. What is the bottleneck: activation, retention, monetization, distribution, market size, or product love?
5. What is the fastest experiment that could improve the bottleneck?
6. Does the market become bigger because of a recent technological/social/regulatory shift?
7. Are we measuring actual growth or vanity motion?

## Useful growth metric patterns

| Business type | Better metric | Usually weaker metric |
|---|---|---|
| SaaS | Weekly active teams, retained paid accounts, expansion revenue | Signups, demos booked |
| Consumer | Repeat usage, retention cohort, organic invites | Downloads, pageviews |
| Marketplace | Liquidity, repeat transactions, supply-demand match rate | Total users, listings |
| Protocol/web3 | Valuable repeated usage, transaction volume tied to utility, retained contributors | Token price, Discord count, speculative volume |
| Dev tools | Weekly active projects, production usage, retained deploys/API calls | GitHub stars, one-off installs |

## Office-hours rule

If we cannot name the metric that should compound, the company is not yet being managed as a startup.
